## 1. Positioning and messaging

### Positioning statement

> For **curious adults who want to really understand a topic** that no
> course covers well (too niche, too new, too scattered, or not at their
> level), **Wondersity** is an **AI tutor that builds a short, interactive course
> on anything you name**. Each lesson is reviewed and fact-checked before you
> see it, and the next one adapts to your mistakes. **Unlike ChatGPT or
> Gemini**, which answer a question and move on, Wondersity plans the whole path,
> makes you practise every idea, grades what you understood, and picks up
> where you left off.

### One-line pitch

**Name any topic. Get a short, interactive course that checks its facts and
adapts to what you get wrong.**

Variants for different lengths:
- 5 words: *A real course on anything.*
- Tweet length: *Type any topic. In under a minute you get a course plan; lesson 1 is ready right after. Ten-minute lessons you do, not read, each fact-checked before you see it.*

### "Why not just ask ChatGPT?" (the answer to use everywhere)

> ChatGPT is great at answering. Learning is different: you need a plan,
> you need to practise, and you need to find out what you got wrong. Wondersity
> plans the whole course first, keeps each lesson to one idea, makes you
> sort, predict, solve and trace instead of reading, grades your first answer
> to each question, and builds the next lesson around your mistakes. Every
> lesson is also reviewed by a second model, its arithmetic is recomputed in
> code, and its key claims are checked against web sources, which are listed
> at the end.

Rules for this answer:
- **Show it, don't claim it.** The fastest proof is a 20-second
  side-by-side video: the same topic in ChatGPT (a wall of text) and in
  Wondersity (plan, then a scene, an activity and a check). Make this video before
  launch.
- **Don't overclaim accuracy.** Say "checked against web sources, with
  sources listed", never "100% accurate" or "verified by experts." The FAQ
  already says obscure topics are where the AI knows least; keep that
  honesty in marketing.
- **Don't attack ChatGPT.** Most of our users use it daily. Frame Wondersity as
  the tool for the case ChatGPT handles badly: learning something properly
  over a few days.

### Messaging pillars

| Pillar | Message | Proof on the page or in the product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. The course nobody made** | Any topic, at your level: a philosopher's method, a forgotten number system, the finance behind a startup. | The topic box; the marquee of real sample topics; the course plan opening in under a minute |
| **2. You do, not read** | Every idea comes with something to do: sort, predict, drag a graph, solve, trace code. | The live activity demos on the landing page ("every card works") |
| **3. Checked before you see it** | A second model scores each lesson, arithmetic is recomputed in code, claims are checked against sources, and the sources are listed. | The Quality section; the sources on each lesson's finish screen |
| **4. It remembers you** | Your mistakes shape the next lesson, and the course picks up where you left off. | The tutor note after a quiz; the course map with saved progress |
| **5. Ten minutes, beautifully** | Short lessons with painted scenes and a guide who speaks: Socrates for the Socratic method. | The scenes and voice in the lesson player |

Lead with pillars 1 and 2 to attract people, use 3 to win trust (especially
against "AI makes things up"), and 4 and 5 to keep them.

### Tagline options

1. **Learn anything, properly.** (clear, broad; recommended for the
   homepage)
2. **The course nobody made. Made for you.** (fits current positioning;
   good for ads and video hooks)
3. **Not a chat. A course.** (sharpest contrast with ChatGPT; good for
   the comparison video)
4. **Any topic. Ten minutes. Actually learned.**
5. **Curiosity, with a plan.**

Test 1-3 as video hooks and ad headlines in weeks 1-4 (section 5) and keep
the winner by click-to-signup rate.

## 2. Ideal customer profile (beachhead)

From MARKET_RESEARCH section 4: **Segment A, curious adults who already pay
to learn.**

| Attribute | ICP |
| --- | --- |
| Age | 22-50, centred on 25-40 |
| Location and language | US first, then UK, Canada, Australia, Ireland (English only; Wondersity writes in English) |
| Behaviour | Listens to history, philosophy or science podcasts and watches explainer YouTube weekly; uses ChatGPT or Gemini; has paid for at least one of Brilliant, MasterClass, Duolingo Plus or Max, Blinkist, Nebula, Audible or a Udemy course in the last 12 months |
| Trigger moments | Just finished a podcast, book, film or documentary and wants to go deeper; New Year's resolutions; a trip (learning a place's history); a new interest picked up from a friend |
| Topics | History, philosophy, conceptual science and maths, economics, music theory, art history, some practical-intellectual topics (personal finance concepts, statistics) |
| Device | Phone and laptop; Wondersity is web-only, so prioritise mobile web quality |
| Job to be done | "When something catches my curiosity, help me actually understand it in a few short sessions, without having to design the course myself, and let me know I got it." |
| Not the ICP (for now) | Under-18s; exam crammers who need their syllabus; people who want certificates; teams buying for employees |

**Disqualifiers we'll watch for in interviews:** wants only passive
listening (they're better served by podcasts); won't do 10 minutes on a
phone; distrusts AI on principle.

## 3. Pricing and packaging

### The proposed pricing, checked against evidence

Proposed (from `ECONOMICS.md`): free tier (plan any course + its first 2
lessons), $19.99/month, $119.99/year, ~60 lessons/month fair use.

| Evidence | What it says about the proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Brilliant ~$162/yr, ~$25-28/mo ([e-student](https://e-student.org/brilliant-org-review/)); Duolingo Max ~$168/yr ([dealnews](https://www.dealnews.com/features/duolingo/cost/)); MasterClass $120-240/yr ([Upskillwise](https://upskillwise.com/masterclass-cost/)); Speak ~$84-120/yr ([AIVario](https://aivario.com/tools/speak)) | **$119.99/yr is in range** for premium adult learning and undercuts Brilliant. Supports the price. |
| Oboe: free unlimited course generation, $15/mo ($144/yr), Pro $40/mo ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/10/oboe-raises-16-million-from-a16z-for-its-ai-powered-course-generation-platform/)) | The closest rival is **cheaper monthly ($15 vs $19.99)** and more generous for free. Wondersity must justify the premium with interaction and trust, or match at around $14.99. |
| Median education subscription price: **$9.99/mo, $44.99/yr** ([RevenueCat 2026, Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)) | Most education apps charge much less. $119.99 is a premium price and needs strong activation to hold. **Challenges the price.** |
| 59-66% of education subscriptions sold are annual (same source) | **Leading with yearly is right.** |
| Education trial users have 50.4% higher 12-month LTV than direct buyers ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/education-app-subscription-benchmarks/)); about half of education trials last 5-9 days ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)) | **Add a 7-day trial**, not just a free tier. |
| Freemium converts ~2-5% ([Artisan Growth](https://www.artisangrowthstrategies.com/blog/freemium-conversion-rate-benchmarks)); opt-in (no card) trials ~9-18% and card-required trials ~31-49% ([VoxBooster compilation](https://voxbooster.com/blog/free-trial-conversion-statistics-2026/), secondary, mostly SaaS) | A card-required trial after a small free taste likely converts far better than freemium alone. |
| Cost per lesson ~$0.27-0.33 (`ECONOMICS.md`) | At **60 lessons/month a yearly subscriber costs ~$16-20 against ~$9.70 of revenue**. The 60-lesson cap loses money on yearly plans. **Challenges the cap.** |

### Recommendation

| Plan | Price | What's included | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Free** | $0 | Plan up to 3 courses; take the first 2 lessons of your first course (and lesson 1 of later ones) | Enough to reach the "aha" moment (lesson 1 finished, lesson 2 adapted to mistakes) while capping free cost at about **$0.70-1.00 per signup** (**Estimate**: plan + world painting ~$0.07 + 2 lessons × $0.27-0.33). Don't write lessons ahead past the free limit. |
| **7-day trial** | $0, card required | Everything in yearly | Converts a motivated free user. Ask at the natural point: after lesson 2, when lesson 3 is about to be built around their mistakes. |
| **Yearly** (lead with this) | **$119.99** (show as $10/month) | ~30 fully illustrated lessons a month, then unlimited lessons with lighter staging (no new paintings) | Keeps the premium anchor below Brilliant. The illustrated cap protects margin (see below). |
| **Monthly** | **$19.99** | Same as yearly | Makes yearly look like 50% off. Test $14.99 if monthly churn is high or Oboe pricing hurts conversion. |
| **Founding member** (first 90 days only) | **$79.99 first year**, renews at $119.99 | Same as yearly | Rewards early users, gives real price data, and avoids anchoring the brand at a low permanent price. |
| Single course (optional, later) | $9.99 | One full course | Only if interviews show many "I'd buy one course, not subscribe" users. Don't launch with it; it splits the message. |

**Fair use, revised:** replace "about 60 lessons a month" with "**30
illustrated lessons a month (about one a day), then unlimited lessons with
text, activities and voice but no new paintings**." Estimated cost of a
lesson without new paintings and with cheaper staging: ~$0.15-0.20
(**Estimate**, from `ECONOMICS.md`: paintings ~$0.09, staging ~$0.045). A
heavy yearly user at 60 lessons would then cost ~$13-15 a month instead of
~$18. Ship the cheaper staging in `ECONOMICS.md` (Luna, 1-2 scenes) before
paid launch. That is what makes yearly plans profitable for typical users.

**Price tests to run (once activation is proven):**
1. Yearly $119.99 vs $79.99, shown to new users at random. Measure trial
   starts × trial-to-paid × revenue per visitor, not conversion alone.
2. Monthly $19.99 vs $14.99.
3. Free tier only vs free tier + card-required 7-day trial.

Don't run price tests with fewer than ~200 paywall views per arm. Before
that, use founding-member preorders as the price signal.

**Payments:** Stripe on the web. No in-app purchase until there is a native
app. If an iOS app comes later, link out to web checkout in the US
(allowed since May 2025, [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/apple-anti-steering-ruling-monetization-strategy)).

### Concrete first actions per channel

**1. Short-form video**
- Build a recording workflow: a screen capture of a real lesson step (scene
  plus guide voice plus one activity), 15-35 seconds, captioned, with the
  hook in the first second ("Socrates will now teach you the Socratic
  method").
- Content formats to rotate:
  1. *"I asked AI to teach me X"*: plan reveal → one activity → the check.
  2. *ChatGPT vs Wondersity*: the same topic side by side.
  3. *"Guess the answer"*: show a check and let commenters answer, then reveal.
  4. *Viewer requests*: "comment a topic and I'll make the course", then
     reply with a video.
- Cadence: 1 video a day for 30 days across TikTok, Instagram Reels and
  YouTube Shorts (same file). Track views → profile clicks → signups per
  video with a UTM link per platform.
- Every video points to a topic landing page (`/learn/<topic>` or similar)
  that starts that exact course after signup (`useStartCourse` already keeps
  the topic through signup).

**2. Communities**
- List 25 communities where the ICP gathers: subject subreddits (history,
  philosophy, AskScience-adjacent, learnmath, musictheory, etc.), plus 5-10
  Discords for history and philosophy podcasts. Read each one's rules;
  many ban self-promotion, so only post where allowed or in designated
  threads.
- Post value first: a free course on a topic the community cares about,
  shared as a public link, with an open request for corrections. ("I built
  an AI tutor and made a 9-lesson course on the Peloponnesian War. Can
  historians here tear it apart?") Corrections improve quality and build
  credibility.
- Recruit research participants (MARKET_RESEARCH 6.1) through the same
  posts.

**3. Creators and affiliates**
- Shortlist 40 micro-creators (5k-100k followers) in history, philosophy,
  science and maths explainers. Send a personalised course made from one of
  their own videos or episodes ("Here's a 6-lesson course companion to your
  episode on Marcus Aurelius").
- Offer: 30% of revenue for 12 months per referred subscriber (in line with
  Teachable and Thinkific at 30% recurring,
  [Tapfiliate](https://tapfiliate.com/blog/best-creator-affiliate-programs-to-join-gp/)),
  a free account, and a co-branded course page. Add a flat fee only for
  creators with proven conversion.
- Build: referral codes and attribution (Stripe coupon + `?ref=` stored on
  signup) before outreach.

**4. Launch platforms**
- Product Hunt: prepare 3 weeks ahead (assets, a 60-second video,
  first-comment story, a list of supporters to notify). Launch on a Tuesday
  or Wednesday.
- Show HN: post a technical write-up, not the product page. For example:
  "How we stop an AI tutor from teaching wrong facts: arithmetic recomputed
  in code, claims checked against search, a separate reviewer model" (the
  material is in `HARNESS.md` and `CHANGES.md`). Plain language; avoid
  sounding like an ad ([Show HN playbook](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/show-hn-launch-playbook-technical-founders-2026)).
- Indie Hackers and relevant newsletters ("AI tools" roundups): submit the
  same week.

**5. SEO**
- Make a small number of **public, quality-gated sample courses** shareable
  without login: start with 30, the most-requested topics from the waitlist
  survey. Each gets a real page (plan, lesson 1 playable, sources).
- Each page targets "learn [topic]" / "[topic] explained" / "[topic] course"
  long-tail queries. Add structured data (`Course` schema).
- Don't generate thousands of pages. Grow from 30 to ~200 only if pages
  index and bring signups after 8-12 weeks.
- Aim to be **cited in AI answers** too: clear, factual pages with sources
  are what AI Overviews and chatbots quote.

**6. Newsletters and podcasts (later)**
- After 90 days, if paywall conversion is known: buy 2-3 test placements in
  history and philosophy newsletters or podcasts at under $1,000 each, with
  a dedicated code.

**7. Paid social (tests only)**
- One $300-500 test in weeks 3-4: the best-performing organic video as a
  Spark Ad (TikTok) and a Reels ad, optimised for signups, not installs.
  The goal is learning which hook converts, not growth.

**8. Partnerships**
- One conversation each with a history podcast network and a public
  library digital-learning programme in the first 90 days. The ask: a
  co-branded course series for their audience.

**9. Schools**
- None for now. Add to the terms that Wondersity is for adults (18+, or 13+ with
  a parent's consent where the law allows).

## 6. Metrics

### North star

**Weekly returning learners (WRL):** learners who finish at least 2 lessons
in a calendar week, not counting the week they signed up.

Why: it combines value (lessons finished, with checks answered), habit
(more than one lesson a week) and retention (excludes the first-week burst).
Revenue follows from it, and it can't be inflated by signups alone.
It's computable today from `lessons.completed_at` and `auth.users.created_at`.

### Activation

**Activated learner:** finishes lesson 1 (every check answered) *and*
starts lesson 2 within 7 days of signup.

Lesson 2 is where the product's difference shows: it is written around the
learner's mistakes in lesson 1. Track the "aha" moment as a leading
indicator: finishing the first activity inside lesson 1.

### Funnel targets

| Step | Target (90 days) | Benchmark or basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landing visitor → signup | 6-10% from warm traffic (video, community, creators); 2-4% cold | **Assumption**; no reliable public benchmark for consumer web education signup found |
| Signup → course planned | ≥ 85% | **Assumption**: a topic box with signup that keeps the topic should rarely fail |
| Course planned → lesson 1 finished | ≥ 50% | **Assumption**. Compare: education Day-1 retention ~27-42% ([GetStream](https://getstream.io/blog/app-retention-guide/), [Semnexus](https://semnexus.com/day-1-day-7-day-30-retention-benchmarks-app-category-2026)) |
| **Activation** (lesson 1 done + lesson 2 started within 7 days), share of signups | **≥ 35%** | **Assumption**; from the research threshold of 40% of lesson-1 finishers |
| Signup → trial start | ≥ 8% | Education median download-to-trial 6.5% ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)) |
| Trial → paid (card-required, 7 days) | ≥ 35% | Card-required trials ~31-49% ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/free-trial-conversion-statistics-2026/), secondary, mostly SaaS) |
| Signup → paid by day 30 | ≥ 3-4% | Freemium typical 2-5% ([Artisan Growth](https://www.artisangrowthstrategies.com/blog/freemium-conversion-rate-benchmarks)) |
| Share of paid on yearly | ≥ 60% | Education 59-66% ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)) |

### Retention targets

### Unit economics used here

| With cheaper staging (~$0.20/lesson, **Estimate**) | ~$6.70 (69%) | ~$16.10 (84%) |

**Gross-profit lifetime value (Estimates):**
- Yearly: 12 × $5.20 = $62 in year 1, plus 24% renewal
  ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/average-subscription-renewal-rates-by-app-category))
  × $62 ≈ $15, so **≈ $77**.
- Monthly: at 12% monthly churn (**Assumption**), ~8 months × $14.60, so
  **≈ $120**. At 20% churn, ~5 months, so ≈ $73.
- Blended 60% yearly / 40% monthly: **≈ $75-95**.
- Minus the free users each subscriber carries: at 3-4% conversion, ~25-33
  free signups × ~$0.80 (**Estimate**) ≈ **$20-27**.
- **Contribution LTV per paying subscriber ≈ $50-70** (**Estimate**).

**Cash payback is better than LTV suggests:** a yearly subscriber pays
~$116 upfront, so a channel with CAC under ~$60 pays back in cash on day one
for yearly buyers. The gross-profit LTV is still the ceiling on what we can
spend.

**Rule: keep CAC per paying subscriber under ~$35 (half of contribution LTV)
until retention is measured.**

## 8. Risks, mitigations and when to change course

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **"ChatGPT is good enough."** Learners try one lesson and go back to free chat. | High | Fatal | Lead with what chat can't do (activities, grading, adaptation, sources). Measure lesson-2 return. If it fails, find out in interviews which part of the lesson felt like chat. |
| **Oboe or Google ships the same thing, free or cheaper.** Oboe has $20M ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/10/oboe-raises-16-million-from-a16z-for-its-ai-powered-course-generation-platform/)); Google has Learn Your Way ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/learn-your-way/)). | High | High | Compete on depth (interactive blocks, verification, the course world and guide), not on "AI makes courses." Own a niche first (humanities with interaction). Build an audience (email list, video following) that doesn't depend on being the only option. |
| **A public factual error.** | Medium | High | Exact accuracy claims, a "report an error" button, a fast-fix promise, a public error log, a concierge audit in the first 100 courses. |
| **Low retention after the first course.** | High (category norm) | High | Next-course recommendations, spaced review of mistakes, lifecycle email, streaks tied to real completions. |
| **Free usage costs more than planned.** | Medium | Medium | Free tier limited to 2 lessons of the first course; no writing ahead past the limit; daily spend caps; batched invites. |
| **Heavy users on yearly plans lose money.** | Medium | Medium | 30 illustrated lessons a month, then lighter staging; cheaper staging shipped before paid launch. |
| **Model price or quality changes.** | Medium | Medium | Multi-model setup via OpenRouter; monthly re-run of `scripts/sample-lessons.ts` and `judge-lessons.ts`. |
| **Search traffic shrinks under AI Overviews.** | High | Low-medium | SEO is rank 5, not the base. Prioritise channels we control (video following, email list). |
| **Regulatory (minors, EU).** | Low if adult-only | High | Adults only; age confirmation; DPAs with vendors; no EU school sales; legal review before any "assessment" or credential claims. |
| **Founder time spread too thin** (product + daily video + community + outreach). | High | Medium | Two channels at a time; drop the bottom two at week 12. |

### What would make us change course

Decide at the 90-day review (week 13, Feb 8-14, 2027), or earlier if a
signal is very clear:

| Signal | Threshold | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activation (lesson 1 done + lesson 2 started) | Under 20% of signups after two rounds of onboarding fixes | The core loop isn't landing. Stop channel work and fix the lesson experience. Interview drop-offs weekly. |
| Lesson-2 return among lesson-1 finishers | Under 25% | Learners don't see a reason to continue. Test a shorter course (3-4 lessons), stronger tutor notes, email nudges. |
| Segment B beats A | B's activation and trial rates are ≥ 1.5× A's | Switch the beachhead to professionals: messaging toward "learn it for work this week", LinkedIn and SEO channels, team plans. |
| Willingness to pay | Under 2% signup → paid, and preorders under 10 from 300 activated | Test $79.99/yr and $9.99/mo. If still low, consider a one-off course purchase or a creator-led model (creators sell Wondersity-built courses). |
| Error rate | More than 1 substantive error per 20 lessons on common topics | Pause growth; narrow to the subjects where verification works well. |
| CAC | No channel under $35 per paying subscriber after 3 tests | Stay on organic and affiliates; don't take on the growth plan. |
| Competitive shock | Google or OpenAI ships free any-topic interactive courses | Double down on the parts they won't build: the course world and guide, community courses, and creator partnerships. |

If activation, return and willingness to pay all clear their thresholds,
move to the growth plan in section 7 and start building the mobile app,
with web checkout.
